# Data-Driven Learning Algorithm (DDQN) for Autonomous Modulation and Coding in Acoustic Communications [with Python code]

Abstract: With the development of underwater acoustic adaptive communication systems, energy-efficient transmission has become an important topic in underwater acoustic communication. Due to the uniqueness of underwater environments, the transmitting nodes almost always have outdated channel state information (CSI), which leads to low energy efficiency. In this paper, we fully utilize bidirectional links and propose an Adaptive Modulation and Coding (AMC) plan aimed at maximizing long-term energy efficiency through jointly scheduled coding rate, modulation order, and transmission power for a single link. To address the complexity of underwater acoustic channels, we introduce a bit error rate estimation method based on deep neural networks. This network utilizes fixed pilots from the feedback link for channel estimation, feature extraction, and bit error rate estimation. Subsequently, we design a channel classification method based on the estimated BER and model UWA channel as a finite-state Markov chain (FSMC) with unknown transition probabilities. Therefore, we represent the AMC problem as a Markov decision process (MDP) and solve it through a reinforcement learning framework. Considering large state-action pairs, we propose a dual deep Q network (DDQN)-based solution. Simulation results show that the algorithm outperforms the fixed MCS algorithm while maintaining channel information states, achieving near-optimal energy efficiency.
Keywords: Acoustic Communication; Adaptive Modulation and Coding; Bit Error Rate Estimation; Channel Classification; Dual Deep Q Network
I. Introduction
Underwater robots, as key mobile nodes for underwater observation and development, require continuous information exchange with other platforms or nodes to achieve information sharing. Underwater information transmission, as an indispensable key technology for the development of underwater robots, has become one of the most active and rapidly developing research fields in recent years [1]. Since acoustic communication is the only way to achieve reliable communication over medium and long distances underwater, it has always been a priority in underwater communication research.
To achieve efficient underwater acoustic communication, it is necessary to establish reliable data communication links between underwater nodes. In practice, most underwater nodes are typically powered by batteries. Since the nodes are deployed in the water, replacing batteries is both time-consuming and expensive. Therefore, long-term energy efficiency is an important criterion for evaluating the performance of communication systems.
Due to the unique characteristics of the underwater environment, UWA channels are often considered one of the most challenging communication channels [3,4]. The characteristic of UWA channels is rapid time-variation, meaning that the channel state changes rapidly with time and position. This dynamic nature makes it difficult to achieve long-term energy-efficient transmission using fixed modulation. Therefore, it is necessary to adopt a dynamic transmission strategy based on channel state information.
Adaptive Modulation and Coding (AMC) technology tracks the channel dynamics and switches among a set of predefined modulation and coding schemes (MCS) adaptively to achieve the most efficient transmission. Compared to traditional fixed MCS underwater acoustic communication systems, AMC systems can obtain better energy efficiency. In an AMC system, the transmitter selects the optimal transmission rate or energy consumption while meeting the bit error rate (BER) requirements. Therefore, the transmitter needs to know the BER of various MCS under a certain UWA channel. For ground wireless communication, the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is usually selected as a metric directly related to BER [5,6]. However, unlike wireless communication channels, underwater acoustic channels have characteristics such as large delay spread, fast time-variation, severe Doppler effect, and limited available bandwidth. Currently, there is no widely accepted mathematical model for underwater channels. Thus, it is difficult to find the relationship between channel parameters and BER performance, making it challenging to use model-based methods. Moreover, the time-varying nature of underwater acoustic channels is often unknown, which also increases the difficulty of AMCUWA communication.
In this paper, we propose a data-driven approach to AMC UWA communication. The transmitter node first estimates the BER of all MCSs using the pilot signal received from the feedback link. Then, based on the estimated BER, the transmitter node classifies the feedback UWA channel. The transmitter node selects the optimal MCS for the next transmission based on the classification result and its own system state. The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:
(1) We propose a BER estimation method based on deep neural networks (DNN). For each transmission, a fixed pilot signal is sent, and the received pilot signal is directly used as the input to the DNN. Preprocessing of the network error rate improves the performance of the network. The proposed network performs all functions, including channel estimation, feature extraction, and BER estimation.
(2) Unlike existing methods that use channel characteristics to classify underwater acoustic channels, this paper directly uses the estimated error rates of all MCSs to classify the feedback underwater acoustic channels. A channel classification criterion based on the error rates of all MCSs is designed. Based on the channel classification method, we can model the underwater acoustic channel as a finite-state Markov chain (FSMC) with unknown transition probabilities.
(3) We represent the adaptive modulation and coding problem as a Markov decision process (MDP) and solve it through a reinforcement learning (RL) framework. Considering the unknown transition probability of the channel state and the large number of state-action pairs, we propose a method based on a dual deep Q network (DDQN) to solve this problem.
(4) We use a statistical underwater acoustic channel model to generate complex value underwater acoustic channels for evaluation of the proposed performance. Simulation results show that the proposed BER estimation method, channel classification method, and DDQN-based AMC scheme have good performance. The proposed scheme achieves near-optimal energy efficiency and perfect channel information.
II. Relevant works
AMC communication is a research hotspot in the field of hydroacoustic communication. In 2000, Stojanovic et al. proposed a simple adaptive modulation scheme for hydroacoustic communication [7]. Multipath, Doppler spread, and SNR measurements were used to select the optimal phase-coherent (PSK and QAM) and non-coherent (FSK) modulations. In the referenced paper [8], channel capacity and equalized SNR were used as metrics for adaptive measurement. The literature [9] introduced a variable rate adaptive modulation system based on instantaneous SNR information. Channel fading effects were modeled as an Nakagami-m distribution, where the parameter m was estimated over the entire experiment or smaller time windows depending on the variability of SNR. Stojanovic et al. proposed an adaptive orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) modulation based on predictive channel for maximizing system throughput under target average bit error rate (BER) [10]. Zhou et al. used channel estimation and effective SNR after channel decoding to select MCS schemes in the UWA OFDMA system [11, 12]. Reference [13] proposed a method for adaptive design of OFDM transmission systems using long-term stability of channel state information (CSI) second-order statistics. Based on statistical information of channel state information, analytical expressions for SINR across subcarriers achieving target bit error performance were derived. On this basis, an adaptive coding and bit power loading algorithm was proposed. This method relies on the accuracy of CSI. Inaccurate CSI can lead to inappropriate modulation schemes, which in turn results in low communication efficiency. Reference [14] proposed an adaptive UWA OFDMA system based on deep neural networks for CSI prediction.
The aforementioned work only considers the instantaneous state of the channel, without considering its temporal correlation. In fact, underwater acoustic channels are time-correlated, and some research works have exploited this characteristic. In [27], Wang et al. proposed an adaptive transmission scheme for point-to-point UWA communication systems. The characteristics of the channel within each epoch are modeled as an unknown Markov process by a composite Nakagami-lognormal distribution with evolving parameters. Subsequently, the adaptive transmission problem is transformed into an MDP and solved using an RL algorithm. In [28], a single input multiple output (SIMO) acoustic communication system based on Dyna-Q algorithm for adaptive modulation was proposed. The channel state is characterized by the effective signal-to-noise ratio (ESNR) after time-reversal processing and the channel estimation based on decision feedback equalization. Reference [29] introduced an AMCUWA communication based on RL. This paper adopts the Q-learning algorithm. In [30], the author further proposed an RL-based AMC scheme for acoustic image communication. Reference [31] presented an adaptive modulation method for remote acoustic communication. The author selected the optimal modulation from four predefined modulations based on the preliminary assessment of the channel. By training a classifier using an acoustic propagation model, the author identified the important features of the channel, thereby reducing sensitivity to mismatches in environmental information. In [32], the author proposed a TAS-DQN method to select the optimal transmitting frequency and power for individual links to achieve optimal energy efficiency.
III. System Model
We consider a point-to-point acoustic communication system consisting of a pair of transmitters and receivers. Both nodes can send signals. Before each transmission, the transmitter node has already received feedback signals from the receiver node. The feedback signals contain the results of previous transmissions. The feedback link is usually encoded at a low rate to ensure no errors. Considering the dynamic characteristics of the acoustic channel, we model the acoustic channel as an FSMC with unknown transition probabilities. Therefore, there is a relationship between the feedback channel and the forward channel.
The transmitter node selects the MCS for the current transmission based on the feedback transmission results and its status. In this paper, the MCS includes coding rate, modulation order, and transmit power. Subsequently, the transmitter encodes, modulates, and sends the information data according to the selected MCS. Here, we employ single carrier frequency domain equalization (SC-FDE) modulation [33-36]. The SC-FDE system adds a cyclic prefix (CP) at the transmitter to convert linear convolution into circular convolution, and uses FFT/IFFT to reduce computational complexity. Due to its advantages of having a lower peak-to-average power ratio and being insensitive to frequency offset, it has become a promising method for addressing intersymbol interference and Doppler spread in underwater acoustic communication. Without doubt, the proposed method can be extended to other communication schemes.

The transmitter classifies the UWA channel according to the method mentioned in the previous section. Reflecting on our objective in (13), we aim to maximize energy efficiency by selecting the optimal transmission parameters {rn, Mn, Pn}. Since we model the time-varying channel as an FSMC, problem (13) can be represented as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). Due to the unknown channel state transition probabilities and BER expressions in (11), traditional optimization methods or dynamic programming (DP) cannot solve this problem. Therefore, we adopt deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to address (13).
RL [42] is an important branch of machine learning. It consists of four key concepts: agent, state, action, and reward. The agent makes actions based on its state and moves from one state to another. These actions are evaluated by the corresponding rewards and trained agents to maximize cumulative rewards. Therefore, the agent learns from interactions with the environment. Due to the lack of prior knowledge about channels, we use unmodeled RL methods, Q-learning.
